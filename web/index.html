Filters

Ordering. A posting whose closing date is inside the pin window sits above everything else, soonest first, whatever the sort says — but only if it is on the shortlist (apply now or strong). A closing date says a posting expires; it says nothing about whether you want it, so pinning every dated card promoted the ones nothing had read above the ones worth opening. Everything else keeps its place in the sort you picked, and Closing date still orders purely by deadline — that is where to look for anything further down that is about to close. The pin window is the pin control — set it to nothing to sort purely by the spine you picked.

Closing dates are read, never guessed. Two sources publish one as a field — MyCareersFuture on every Singapore row, which is 98% of the dated cards here, and JobStream on every Swedish ad. Everything else has none. They are deliberately not mined out of descriptions: hundreds of Swedish ads carry the sentence “tjänsten kan tillsättas innan sista ansökningsdag” with no date in it, and Ashby prints “unless a specific application deadline is stated” on everything it hosts. A guessed date would pin the wrong card to the top of this page for weeks.

Posted dates are not all equally known. Greenhouse, Ashby, Workable, Lever and JobStream publish a timestamp. Workday publishes a sentence — Posted 30+ Days Ago is a floor, not a date. BambooHR and Personio publish nothing, so those fall back to the day we first saw the posting, an upper bound. Anything approximate is dotted and says what it really knows on hover.

Stacking collapses a group of three or more into one cell that keeps the rank of its most urgent member, so a stack can never bury a posting that is closing. Group by firm to see who is hiring, by role and place to see one kind of job in one city.

Two stages, and only the first one removes anything. Stage one is a gate: a nurse, a welder, a Medical AI Specialist are not distant quant roles but other professions, so they never reach this page. It runs off JobStream's own occupation taxonomy where there is one — 15 of its 21 fields can never hold a quant job — and off a short list of unambiguous occupation words where there is not. Stage two is everything in the rail, and it only ranks: unknown is a real value with its own checkbox, because a posting nothing has decided about must stay reachable.

Most postings with “Trader” in the title are not quant trading. Agency MBS Trader, Precious Metals Trader, Associate Trader, Fixed Income — real seats, and not this job. Trading style splits them from Quantitative, Systematic and Algorithmic Trader, read from the title alone because a body belonging to a trading firm says “systematic” about the firm.

Nothing is deleted anywhere. The gate is a tag with the evidence that set it; the rows stay in the database, and list --exclude off_industry shows exactly what it ate. Every build prints the count.

A red preset in the rail hides without judging. Hide credit risk and Hide pure trader roles remove a match from this browser's view only -- nothing is tagged, nothing is written anywhere, and the lexicon still calls the work relevant. They exist for postings that are a real, correctly-graded fit and just not wanted twice. Both start off; clicking one leaves a crumb above the grid saying what is hidden, and clicking that crumb brings it back.

Saved lives in this browser only, keyed on the posting id, and comes back with the Saved preset. Reject is one click for "not this line of work" -- it takes the posting off the board immediately and comes back with the Rejected preset; click it again to undo. The two dropdowns under each card's actions do the same thing more precisely -- correct relevance and seniority by hand when the tagger got one wrong or left it unknown -- and the override sorts, filters and displays as the corrected value from then on, in this browser, immediately. Every correction made here also goes to a small Lambda that holds it until python -m quantscraper corrections (or a normal daily run) pulls it into quantscraper/labels.csv on the machine that owns that file -- no download, no manual merge, just a delay until the next pull instead of an immediate write. Opened over file:// or a bare http.server there is no route to answer that POST at all, and a phone can simply be offline — so a correction that does not get through is kept and retried on the next one and on the next page load, and the rail says how many are still waiting. Nothing has to be downloaded or merged by hand.

A rejection sticks across rebuilds now, and across a reposting. Once a Reject reaches labels.csv, the next build drops that posting before the page is written -- so it does not come back in a new browser or on another machine. It also drops any posting with the same fingerprint: same firm, same stated location, same description. One Singapore recruiter reposts the identical advertisement every five days under a fresh id, and rejecting it once is meant to be the end of it.

×N on a card means the same opening was found N times, and it folds two different things. One is an advertisement reposted -- same firm, same place, same text -- where the newest is kept; one Singapore recruiter republishes the identical ad every five days under a fresh id. The location is part of that test on purpose: a firm that sends one description to every office is hiring in every office, so Hong Kong and London stay two cards rather than becoming one.

The other is one job carried by two sources -- a firm's own careers board and a national board that republishes it, which is what a national board is for. Barclays' Quantitative Analytics Associate Off Cycle Internship is on its own Workday board and on MyCareersFuture, and nothing about the two rows matches: different employer identity, different address, and 7,875 characters of description on one against none on the other. So that fold is decided by the firm, the place and the title together, and the firm test is strict — Squarepoint Services Singapore and Squarepoint Ops are one company; Blockchain Capital and Chevron Singapore are not, and neither are the seven different firms advertising a job called Trader in Singapore. The card kept is whichever this board rates higher, because the two copies are read separately and do not always agree, and the closing date comes across with it.
